Case 01

Basko Clean

Website, motion-graphic ad and social media for an industrial cleaning company

Client
Basko Clean, Szczecin. B2B industrial cleaning
My role
Design, front-end code, video, social media
Status
Live website, maintained and updated by me
Tools
Claude Code, React, Canva, video editing

AProblem and context

Basko Clean cleans halls, warehouses, floors, roofs and facades for companies. The people who visit the site are buyers at other businesses. They want to know whether Basko handles their type of site and how to get a price.

The company needed a website, an ad and a steady presence on Instagram, LinkedIn and TikTok.

BMy role and constraints

  • I designed and coded the website. I keep it updated.
  • I made the video ad, run the social profiles, film on job sites and edit the reels.
  • I prepared the profile identity: a vector version of the logo's cleaner figure, profile pictures in light and dark versions, the Instagram bio.
  • Constraint: names and logos of Basko's own clients appear only with their consent.
  • Constraint: a small ad budget.

What AI did / What I did

AI What AI did

  • Claude Code wrote and changed the site's code (React components) from my layout and copy.
  • An AI video model produced a realistic 3D version of the ad.
  • Canva sped up posts and profile graphics.

Me What I did

  • Decided the page structure and put the quote form in the first screen.
  • Rejected the realistic 3D version. It did not fit the brand. I chose flat 2D motion graphics with kinetic type and the logo's cleaner figure as the hero.
  • Filmed on job sites, edited the reels, run three social profiles.
  • Vectorised the logo figure, made light and dark profile pictures, wrote the bio.
CProcess: research → flow → prototype → final
  1. Research. The service range (halls, floors, roofs, facades, parking lots) and before and after photos from real jobs. The range became the service list in the form and the checklist in the reel.
  2. Flow. The visitor sees what Basko cleans, then asks for a quote. The form asks for three things: service, phone number, postcode.
  3. Prototype. Built directly in code with Claude Code: React components rendered in the browser.
  4. Final. Live site in Basko's navy palette with Inter and Plus Jakarta Sans. The ad went through two directions: realistic 3D from AI, then flat 2D motion graphics. The second one shipped.
DStates and edge cases I planned for
  • ConsentClient logos and names stay off the site until each client agrees. The trust section has to work without them.
  • ThemeProfile pictures in light and dark versions, so the figure reads on both app themes.
  • BudgetA small ad budget, so content comes from my own footage on job sites, cut for three platforms.
  • LanguageThe site has a Polish and an English version.

EOutcome, what I learned, what I would do next

The site is live and I keep updating it. I continue to run Basko's social profiles.

Case 02

KSK Pretor

Shooting range and events in Racław near Gorzów. Market research for Germany, a group booking page, a 3D model, an AI agent prototype and video branding.

Client
KSK Pretor, shooting range and events
My role
Research, strategy, UX, 3D, motion
Outputs
18-chapter plan, 59-page owner version, 3D model, film, intro and outro
Tools
ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude Code, three.js, GSAP, Botpress
  • 18chapters in the market entry plan
  • 40+competitors analysed
  • 5personas
  • 4customer journeys
  • 7points where customers drop off
  • 8conversion levers

AProblem and context

KSK Pretor wanted guests from Germany. A German group looks at a range in Poland from very different starting points: an HR team planning a company event, an agency booking for a client, friends, a weapons enthusiast, someone organising a bachelor party.

Each of them plans on a different timeline, from a few days to twelve months. The owner needed to know where these people drop off and what a German landing page must offer.

BMy role and constraints

  • I wrote the market entry plan: positioning, competitors, personas, journeys, drop-off points, levers, landing page requirements, SEO, ads, B2B outbound, KPIs.
  • I cut it into a 59-page version for the owner. Every section answers the same five questions.
  • I built the 3D model, the AI agent prototype and the video branding.
  • Constraint: video assets in two languages, Polish and German.
  • Constraint: brand colours red, black and white.

Four journeys, five personas

Planning time ranges from a few days to twelve months. Drop-off points sit along every journey. The plan answers each one with a conversion lever.

Diagram simplified for the portfolio. Bar lengths and dot positions are illustrative. The full plan names each journey, drop-off and lever.

What AI did / What I did

AI What AI did

  • Perplexity gathered market data with sources. Gemini ran comparisons.
  • ChatGPT drafted and restructured strategy text with me.
  • Claude Code wrote the three.js code for the 3D model and the GSAP animations.
  • Botpress runs the agent prototype.

Me What I did

  • Set the structure of the plan and the five questions every owner section answers.
  • Defined the landing page requirements: packages in EUR, booking, conversion tracking.
  • Designed the 3D interactions and states: safe mode, step-by-step disassembly, photo mode with Instagram formats, lower quality on phones.
  • Directed the motion: the bullet crosses the frame, the logo appears behind it. Polish and German versions.

CProcess: research → flow → prototype → final
  1. Research. Positioning, more than 40 competitors, five personas, SEO, ads and B2B outbound. Market data gathered with Perplexity, with sources.
  2. Flow. Four customer journeys from first trigger to visit, seven drop-off points along them, eight levers to fix them.

Case 03

Livingroom

A restaurant website in Gorzów Wielkopolski, built on my own initiative as a demo for a cold pitch

Client
Livingroom restaurant, Gorzów Wlkp. Not commissioned
My role
Idea, design, code, pitch
Status
Nearly finished demo. Missing piece: the booking form
Tools
Claude Code, HTML, CSS, JS

AProblem and context

I visited Livingroom and found that its website did not match the quality of the place.

Nobody asked me for a redesign. I built a nearly finished site as a demo and used it to start the conversation.

BMy role and constraints

  • Everything: the idea, the design, the code, the pitch.
  • Constraint: no brief and no budget. The demo had to convince on its own.
  • Constraint: the booking form needed an integration with the restaurant's system, which a demo cannot have. That was the missing piece.

The missing piece

Table booking, every state

◆ Concept prototype built for this portfolio

Date, time, party size, details, confirmation. With the states a real booking form has to handle: no free times, validation errors, groups above 8, loading, network errors and success. Use “Show all states” to see each one without clicking through.

Availability and times are sample data, not the restaurant's real schedule. Nothing is sent anywhere.

What AI did / What I did

AI What AI did

  • Claude Code wrote the demo's code from my layout: menu, categories, dish carousel, language switch.
  • Claude Code wrote the booking prototype on this page from my list of steps and states.
  • Content pending Which tool made the storefront and food illustrations.

Me What I did

  • Noticed the problem as a guest and decided to act without a brief.
  • Chose the look: black, gold, serif type, illustrated dishes.
  • Defined the booking flow and every state it must handle, including groups above 8.
  • Pitched the demo to the restaurant.
CProcess: research → flow → prototype → final
  1. Research. I went to the restaurant as a guest and compared the visit with the website.
  2. Flow. Home page, menu, gallery, about, chef, contact, and a booking button on every screen.
  3. Prototype. A nearly finished site in code, used as the pitch itself.
  4. Final. The booking step needs an integration. On this page I show the flow and states as a prototype.
DStates and edge cases I planned for
  • Full dayFully booked days are visible and disabled, with the reason in text.
  • No timesIf a day has no free times, the guest gets the nearest free date in one tap.
  • 9+ guestsGroups above 8 move to an enquiry form. The data already entered carries over.
  • ErrorsEach field says what to fix. An error summary at the top links to each field.
  • NetworkLoading and failure states for times and for sending, with retry that keeps the data.
EOutcome, what I learned, what I would do next

How I work with AI

AI tools are part of every project. I use them to research faster, build faster and try more versions. I decide what the problem is, what ships, and what gets thrown away.

  1. 01

    Understand the problem

    I go where the problem is: the job site, the restaurant. I dictate notes in Wispr Flow.

    Me I set the question.

  2. 02

    Research

    Perplexity for sources, Gemini for comparisons, ChatGPT for structuring strategy, Manifold for marketing and social data.

    AI gathers Me checks sources

  3. 03

    Flow and states

    Before building, I write the steps and every state: empty, loading, error, limit, success.

    Me I write the list.

  4. 04

    Build

    Claude Code turns the list into a working page or prototype. Animations in GSAP and HyperFrames, rendered to MP4 with Playwright. 3D in three.js.

    AI writes code

  5. 05

    Visuals

    Higgsfield and Leonardo AI for images and video, Canva for posts and materials.

    AI generates Me selects

  6. 06

    Review and decide

    I check the result on desktop and phone. I keep what fits the brand and the user and throw away the rest.

    Me I decide.

AI Where AI speeds me up

  • Writing and changing code for pages, prototypes and animations.
  • Collecting market and competitor data with sources.
  • Drafts of long documents and their structure.
  • Image and video variants to compare.
  • Dictation instead of typing.

Me Where the decision stays with me

  • What the problem is and who the user is.
  • Which states and edge cases the product must handle.
  • Whether a visual fits the brand. Basko's realistic 3D ad did not, so I dropped it.
  • What a client's material may show: no client logos without consent, no personal data.
  • Whether the result works. I test it myself on desktop and phone.
