Case 01

Basko Clean

Website, motion-graphic ad and social media for an industrial cleaning company

Client
Basko Clean, Szczecin. B2B industrial cleaning
My role
Design, front-end code, video, social media
Status
Live website, maintained and updated by me
Tools
Claude Code, React; video shot and edited by me

AProblem and context

Basko Clean cleans halls, warehouses, floors, roofs and facades for companies. The people who visit the site are buyers at other businesses. They want to know whether Basko cleans their kind of building and how to get a price.

The company needed a website, an ad and a steady presence on Instagram, LinkedIn and TikTok.

BMy role and constraints

  • I designed and coded the website. I keep it updated.
  • I made the video ad, run the social profiles, film on job sites and edit the reels.
  • I prepared the profile identity: a vector version of the logo's cleaner figure, profile pictures in light and dark versions, the Instagram bio.
  • Constraint: names and logos of Basko's own clients appear only with their consent.
  • Constraint: a small ad budget.

What AI did / What I did

AI What AI did

  • Claude Code writes and updates the site's code (React components) from my layout.
  • An AI video model produced a realistic 3D version of the ad.

Me What I did

  • Decided the page structure and placed the quote form above the fold.
  • Rejected the realistic 3D version. It did not fit the brand. I chose flat 2D motion graphics with kinetic type and the logo's cleaner figure as the hero.
  • Filmed on job sites and edited the reels. I still run the three social profiles.
  • Vectorised the logo figure, made light and dark profile pictures, wrote the bio.
CProcess: research → flow → prototype → final
  1. Input. The service range (halls, floors, roofs, facades, parking lots) and before-and-after photos from real jobs. The range became the service list in the form and the checklist in the reel.
  2. Flow. The visitor sees what Basko cleans, then asks for a quote. The form asks for three things: service, phone number, postcode.
  3. Prototype. Built directly in code with Claude Code: React components rendered in the browser.
  4. Final. Live site in Basko's navy palette with Inter and Plus Jakarta Sans. The ad went through two directions: realistic 3D from AI, then flat 2D motion graphics. I chose the second.
DConstraints I planned for
  • ConsentBasko's client names and logos appear on the site only with each client's consent.
  • ThemeProfile pictures in a light and a dark version.
  • BudgetSmall ad budget. Content is my own footage from job sites, cut for three platforms.
  • LanguageA language switch, Polish and English.
EOutcome, what I learned, what I would do next

The site is live and I keep updating it. I continue to run Basko's social profiles.

KSK Pretor

Shooting range and events in Racław near Gorzów. Market research for Germany, a 3D model, an AI agent prototype and video branding. The German booking page further down is a concept built for this portfolio.

Client
KSK Pretor, shooting range and events
My role
Research, strategy, UX, 3D, motion
Outputs
18-chapter plan, 59-page owner version, 3D model, intro and outro
Tools
ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude Code, three.js, GSAP, Botpress
  • 18chapters in the market entry plan
  • 40+competitors analysed
  • 5personas
  • 4customer journeys
  • 7points where customers drop off
  • 8conversion levers

AProblem and context

KSK Pretor wanted guests from Germany. The research describes five personas with different starting points: an HR team planning a company event, an agency booking for a client, a group of friends, a weapons enthusiast, a bachelor party organiser.

Their planning time ranges from a few days to twelve months. The owner needed to know where these people drop off and what a German landing page must offer.

BMy role and constraints

  • I wrote the market entry plan: positioning, competitors, personas, journeys, drop-off points, levers, landing page requirements, SEO, ads, B2B outbound, KPIs.
  • I cut it down to a 59-page version for the owner. Every section answers the same five questions.
  • I built the 3D model and the video branding.
  • I built an AI agent (chatbot) prototype in Botpress. Content pending: what the agent does
  • Constraint: intro and outro in two languages, Polish and German.
  • Constraint: brand colours red, black and white.

Interaction design
States and interactions of the 3D model
ElementStates and behaviour
Fire selectorClick cycles SAFE → FULL AUTO → SEMI AUTO. Current state shown in the panel.
TriggerIn SAFE the trigger does not fire. In the other modes: trigger moves, muzzle flash, short camera shake.
Charging handleClick: the carrier moves back and returns.
PartsHover highlights the part in red with its name. On touch, a tap shows the name for a few seconds.
DisassemblyDisassemble and assemble buttons, a slider, and step-by-step mode (back / next) through five parts.
Photo modeFive preset shots, a crop frame, PNG export at 1080×1350 (post) or 1080×1920 (story) with the club name. Optional part labels and muzzle flash.
PhonesLower render resolution and shadow quality, no floor reflection.
KeyboardAdded for this portfolio: S fires, Z pulls the charging handle, P switches the selector. Panel buttons work with Tab and Enter.

KSK Pretor · From research to interface

German group booking page

◆ Concept prototype built for this portfolio

The research ended with requirements for a German landing page. This prototype turns them into a booking flow a German group organiser can finish without a phone call.

Research finding → decision in the prototype
From the researchDecision
Landing page requirement: packages priced in EURThree packages with EUR prices, no currency conversion for the visitor

Groups under 12 are sent to the standard booking. Groups over 30 get a custom quote. Prices, the reply time, package names and contents are sample values for the prototype.

My brief for this prototype (PROMPT.md, original Polish)

„Zbudowana z wymagań z researchu: pakiety z ceną w EUR, wybór wielkości grupy (12–30), widoczna cena za osobę i za grupę, rezerwacja terminu, pytanie »Wie haben Sie von uns erfahren?«. Interfejs po niemiecku.”

In English: built from the research requirements: packages priced in EUR, group size 12 to 30, price per person and per group, date booking, the “how did you hear about us” question, German interface.

CProcess: research → flow → prototype → final
  1. Research. Positioning, more than 40 competitors, five personas.
  2. Flow. Four customer journeys, seven drop-off points and eight conversion levers. Then SEO, ads, B2B outbound and KPIs.
  3. Requirements. The German landing page needs packages in EUR, booking and conversion tracking. The booking page in this case is a portfolio concept built from them.
  4. Final. The full plan in 18 chapters, then a 59-page owner version where each section answers the same five questions.
DStates, variants and edge cases I planned for
  • Safety3D model: in SAFE the trigger does not fire, and the panel always shows the selector state.
  • Devices3D model: phones get lower render resolution and shadows and no reflection, to keep the model responsive.
  • FormatsPhoto export in the two Instagram formats, 1080×1350 and 1080×1920.
  • LanguageIntro and outro in Polish and German. A separate “Vielen Dank” card for agencies.
EOutcome, what I learned, what I would do next

The 3D model is live on GitHub Pages. The intro and outro exist in Polish and German. The film is in German.

Livingroom

A restaurant website in Gorzów Wielkopolski, built on my own initiative as a demo for a cold pitch

Client
Livingroom restaurant, Gorzów Wielkopolski. Not commissioned
My role
Idea, design, code, pitch
Status
Nearly finished demo. Missing piece: booking-form integration
Tools
Claude Code, HTML, CSS, JS

AProblem and context

I visited Livingroom and found that its website did not match the quality of the place.

Nobody asked me for a redesign. I built a nearly finished site as a demo for a cold pitch.

BMy role and constraints

  • The idea, the design and the pitch. The code was built with Claude Code.
  • Constraint: no brief and no budget. The demo had to convince on its own.
  • Constraint: before launch, the booking form still needed an integration.

Livingroom · The missing step

Table booking with its states

◆ Concept prototype built for this portfolio

The flow: date, party size, time, details, confirmation. It covers the states a real booking form has to handle: no free times, validation errors, groups above 8, loading, network errors and success. Use “Show all states” to see each one without clicking through.

Availability and times are sample data, not the restaurant's real schedule. Nothing is sent anywhere.

My brief for this prototype (PROMPT.md, original Polish)

„Przepływ: data → godzina → liczba osób → dane → potwierdzenie. Stany: brak wolnych godzin, błędy walidacji przy polach, grupa powyżej 8 osób przechodzi na formularz zapytania, ładowanie, błąd sieci z ponowieniem, sukces z podsumowaniem. Dodaj przełącznik »Show all states«.”

In English: flow from date to confirmation; states for no free times, field errors, groups above 8 moving to an enquiry form, loading, network error with retry, success with a summary; a switch to show all states.

Changed after an AI design review: free tables depend on the size of the group, so the flow asks for guests before the time.

What AI did / What I did

AI What AI did

  • Claude Code wrote the demo's code from my layout: menu, categories, dish carousel, language switch.
  • Claude Code built the booking prototype on this page from my written brief (PROMPT.md in the repository, opens in a new tab, in Polish).

Me What I did

  • Noticed the problem as a guest and decided to act without a brief.
  • Chose the look: black, gold, serif type, illustrated dishes. Illustration tool pending
  • Wrote that brief: five steps and the states the booking must handle, including groups above 8.
  • Built the demo and offered it to the owner.
CProcess: research → flow → prototype → final
  1. Research. I went to the restaurant as a guest and compared the visit with the website.
  2. Flow. Home page, menu, gallery, about, chef, contact, and a booking button on every screen.
  3. Prototype. A nearly finished site in code, used as the pitch itself.
  4. Final. The booking step needs an integration. On this page I show the flow and states as a prototype.
DStates the booking step has to handle (prototype above)
  • Full dayFully booked days are visible and disabled, with the reason in text.
  • No timesIf a day has no free times, the guest gets the nearest free date in one tap.
  • 9+ guestsGroups above 8 move to an enquiry form. The data already entered carries over.
  • ErrorsEach field says what to fix. An error summary at the top links to each field.
  • PhonePolish numbers and numbers with a country code, for guests from abroad.
  • NetworkLoading and failure states for times and for sending, with retry that keeps the data.

How I work with AI

I use AI tools in every project, mostly to research and build faster. Below: my steps, the tools, and one case where you can check the split yourself.

  1. 01

    Start on site

    At the client's job site, or as a guest in the restaurant.

    Me problem definition

  2. 02

    Research

    Perplexity for sources, Gemini for comparisons, ChatGPT for structuring strategy, Manifold for marketing and social data.

    AI gathering Me selection

  3. 03

    Flow and states

    I list the steps and the states before the build. Example: my brief for this portfolio.

    Me the list

  4. 04

    Build

    Claude Code turns the list into a working page or prototype. Animations in GSAP and HyperFrames, rendered to MP4 with Playwright. 3D in three.js.

    AI the code

  5. 05

    Visuals

    Higgsfield and Leonardo AI for images and video, Canva for posts and materials.

    AI variants Me selection

  6. 06

    Review

    Before anything goes to the client, I check it against the brief and the brand.

    Me sign-off

AI Where AI speeds me up

  • Code for pages, prototypes and animations.
  • Market and competitor data with sources.
  • Drafts of long documents and their structure.
  • Image and video variants to compare.
  • Dictation instead of typing.

Me What I decide

  • What the problem is and who the user is.
  • Which states and edge cases the product must handle.
  • Whether a visual fits the brand. Basko's realistic 3D ad did not, so I dropped it.
  • What a client's material may show: no client logos without consent, no personal data.
  • Whether the result is ready for the client.

Case 06

This portfolio

Built with Claude Code from my written brief, then revised over several rounds of automated checks and AI reviews. The brief, the test scripts and the QA log are in the repository (opens in a new tab).

What AI did / What I did on this portfolio

AI What AI did

  • Claude Code wrote the page, the rotating name, the card wheel, the two booking prototypes and the copy drafts.
  • It wrote the test scripts in tests/: screenshots of every section (1440 light, 768 dark, 390 light), a click-through of both booking prototypes with validation errors, a keyboard-only booking in the Livingroom prototype, and an accessibility run in both themes.
  • Separate AI reviewers scored the design and checked every sentence against my facts.

Me What I did

  • Picked the visual direction from a reference: a word rotating on a cylinder, project cards on an arc.
  • Changed the word on the cylinder from “Portfolio” in the reference to my name.
  • Wrote the brief: facts about me and my projects, page structure, the flow and states of the Livingroom booking, the requirements for the KSK booking page.
  • Set the rules: no invented numbers or quotes, every gap marked “Content pending”, no client data.

What the checks and reviews caught QA log (opens in a new tab)

  • LayoutThe “all states” gallery fell apart: buttons were nested inside buttons.
  • KeyboardAfter each step, focus dropped to the page body, so keyboard users lost their place.
  • ScrollSection links landed about 160 px too low: the smooth-scroll library counted the CSS offset twice.
  • PhoneLong headings made the page wider than a 360 px screen.
  • Voice controlDate buttons had spoken names that did not match their visible text.
  • FactsThe fact check flagged sentences that claimed more than my sources. They were rewritten or marked “Content pending”.
  • FlowAI design review: free tables depend on group size, so the booking now asks for guests before the time.
